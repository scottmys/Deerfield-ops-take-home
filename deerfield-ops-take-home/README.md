# Deerfield Intelligence, Director of Operations take home

## Summary

Hiring pipeline speed is the team's biggest gap. David made this call directly when forced to choose between it and vendor procurement. It is backed by a real cost: a four month hire, and a candidate lost to Google.

The ranking holds up independently, too. Hiring is a long lead item. The cost of a slow start does not show up for three to six months, which is why it outranks the vendor gap even though the vendor gap feels more urgent day to day.

The 90 day plan makes hiring the headline. It starts the technical recruiter search in week one, since that search itself has a long lead time. Vendor and contract procurement moves in parallel, not behind hiring, since it carries a live, unresolved financial risk of its own.

The prototype is a vendor and procurement tracker. David named it directly, unprompted, as the artifact he would point to as evidence of success. It is built around two real fast lane criteria gathered from interviews, not invented ones.

## Assumptions

Interviews surfaced one live, unresolved issue during the process itself. Marcus's biotech pilot, three weeks from launch, was missing a formal data sharing agreement establishing the legal right to use the patient data it depends on. This is documented in the gap assessment and the 90 day plan as evidence. It was not resolved as part of this exercise, resolution depends on the portfolio company's own legal team.
