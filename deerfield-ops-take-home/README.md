# Deerfield Intelligence, Director of Operations take home

## Summary

Hiring pipeline speed is the team's biggest gap, David's own call when forced to choose between it and vendor procurement, backed by a real cost, a four month hire and a candidate lost to Google. That ranking holds up independently too, hiring is a long lead item, the cost of a slow start doesn't show up for three to six months, which is why it outranks the vendor gap even though the vendor gap feels more urgent day to day. The 90 day plan makes hiring the headline, starts the technical recruiter search in week one since that search itself has a long lead time, and keeps vendor and contract procurement moving in parallel, since that gap had a live, unresolved financial risk of its own. The prototype is a vendor and procurement tracker, the artifact David named directly as what he'd point to as evidence of success, built around two real fast lane criteria gathered from interviews rather than invented ones.

## Assumptions

Interviews surfaced one live, unresolved issue during the process itself: Marcus's biotech pilot, three weeks from launch, was missing a formal data sharing agreement establishing the legal right to use the patient data it depends on. This is documented in the gap assessment and the 90 day plan as evidence, not resolved as part of this exercise, resolution depends on the portfolio company's own legal team.
