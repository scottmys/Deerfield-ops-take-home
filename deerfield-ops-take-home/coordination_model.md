# Cross functional coordination model

Five specific mechanisms, each with a named meeting, a fixed attendee list, a cadence, an agenda, and a list of what actually gets decided there, not just discussed. The real value this role adds is absorbing coordination that pod leads and David are currently doing themselves on top of their actual jobs, so each mechanism below either replaces a meeting that already exists or replaces an informal habit, like emailing whoever might be free, with a specific owner.

## Summary

| Mechanism | Who is in the room | Cadence | Replaces |
|---|---|---|---|
| DI Hiring Sync | Director of Operations and Elena Rodriguez | Monthly, 30 minutes | The existing David and Elena monthly meeting, David is no longer required to attend |
| DI Finance Sync | Director of Operations and James Okonkwo | Biweekly, 30 minutes | Informal, ad hoc contact whenever James happens to notice something |
| DI Compliance Review | Director of Operations and Sarah Kim | Monthly, 30 to 45 minutes | Sarah reviewing requests cold with no standing check in |
| Novel build check in | Director of Operations, Sarah Kim, and the specific pod lead whose build triggered the flag | Ad hoc, triggered at intake, not scheduled in advance | Sarah finding out about a new build at launch instead of during design |
| Vendor and contract coordination | Director of Operations, coordinating on the requesting pod lead's behalf with the vendor, Legal, the shared part time security reviewer, and Sarah | Per request, as needed, not a standing meeting | Priya or Marcus personally chasing all four parties themselves |

## DI Hiring Sync, Director of Operations and Elena Rodriguez, monthly, 30 minutes

This replaces the meeting that already exists between David and Elena, David does not need to be in this one going forward, that time back is part of what makes hiring the top priority actually pay off. Between meetings, the Director of Operations is Elena's single point of contact for technical requirements and interviewer availability, so she is not fielding separate, uncoordinated asks from Priya and Marcus. If David wants visibility, he gets a short written summary after the sync, not a seat in the meeting.

**Agenda**
1. Review the hiring plan by pod against the 10 to 30 roadmap, note anything that has changed since last month.
2. Walk every open requisition, current stage, time in stage, and what is blocking it.
3. Review every candidate currently in the offer stage specifically, since that is where the candidate was lost to Google.
4. Confirm interviewer availability for the next 30 days across both pods.
5. Any HR capacity issue worth escalating to David.

**Decisions made here**
- Which open requisitions move to active sourcing this month.
- A named owner for unblocking any requisition or offer that has stalled.
- The confirmed interview schedule for the next 30 days.
- Whether to escalate a capacity ask, more of Elena's time or a dedicated recruiter, to David.

## DI Finance Sync, Director of Operations and James Okonkwo, biweekly, 30 minutes

Outside of this meeting, James can check the vendor tracker itself at any time rather than waiting two weeks for an update, which is the real fix to his own complaint, that he is a historian instead of a partner.

**Agenda**
1. Walk the vendor tracker together, new spend since last sync, upcoming renewals, anything auto renewing soon.
2. Review anything sitting in the Legal, Security, or Compliance stage longer than expected.
3. Check the hiring plan against actual budget burn.
4. Confirm cost center tagging is accurate for any new hires since the last sync.

**Decisions made here**
- Whether to cancel, renegotiate, or renew anything approaching its renewal date.
- Whether budget is confirmed for any pending hire or vendor request.
- A named owner for fixing any cost center tagging error found.

## DI Compliance Review, Director of Operations and Sarah Kim, monthly, 30 to 45 minutes

**Agenda**
1. Review everything currently sitting in the fast lane versus full review queue, confirm nothing has been miscategorized against the two gate criteria, cost and data sensitivity.
2. Check whether the shared part time security reviewer's reserved slot is being used or is already backing up.
3. Review any near miss or escalation since the last meeting.
4. Status check on the formal access control matrix.

**Decisions made here**
- Correct the categorization of any borderline request, fast lane or full review.
- Whether the reserved security review slot needs to change in frequency or length.
- Sign off on any pending additions to the access control matrix.

## Novel build check in, ad hoc, triggered at intake

A single yes or no flag on every intake, is this a new type of build or a new type of data use. A yes triggers this check in before formal submission, not after. This is Sarah's own fix, a seat at the table during design rather than a gate at launch, and it is what turns a three day review into a three week one when it is skipped.

**Agenda**
1. The pod lead walks through what is being built and what data or systems it touches.
2. Sarah flags any concern while the design can still change.
3. Agree on what documentation will be needed at formal submission, so nothing is missing later.

**Decisions made here**
- Whether the current design can proceed as is, or needs a change before work continues.
- The specific documentation the pod lead needs to prepare ahead of formal submission.

## Vendor and contract coordination, per request, Director of Operations only

Today a single vendor request touches four parties, the vendor itself, Legal, the shared part time security reviewer, and Sarah in Compliance, and Priya is the one manually chasing all four herself on top of building. This is not a standing meeting, it is a workflow the Director of Operations owns end to end, logged in the vendor tracker so status is visible without anyone having to ask.

**What happens on each request**
1. Check the request against the two gate criteria, cost and data sensitivity.
2. If both gates pass, log it and move straight to approved.
3. If either gate fails, coordinate directly with the vendor, Legal, the security reviewer, and Sarah, so the requesting pod lead does not have to.

**Decisions made here**
- Fast lane or full review, made at intake, not after the fact.
- Final approval, once all required parties have signed off.
